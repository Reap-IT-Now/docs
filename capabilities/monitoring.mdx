Monitoring helps you understand the operational state of your network through the telemetry REAP collects. You can start with an organization view, narrow the scope to a site or device, and inspect the available measurements and evaluated conditions.

This helps your team see where attention is needed and gather context before investigating an incident. Instead of treating a symptom in isolation, you can compare it with device health, interface behavior, and protocol state where those signals are available.

## What you can do

- **Review the organization.** Inspect available health summaries, telemetry coverage, and trends.
- **Focus on a site or device.** Narrow the scope and category to the part of the network relevant to your question.
- **Follow measurements into conditions.** Inspect collected signals, derived features, and evaluated conditions.
- **Check freshness and coverage.** Review available timestamps and reporting information before interpreting a value.

The workspace is called **Telemetry Explorer** and is reached through **Telemetry** in the product navigation.

## Understand the monitoring views

| View | What it shows | How it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signals | Collected telemetry for the selected scope and category. | Inspect the available measurements behind an investigation. |
| Features | Values derived from the collected signals. | Examine the processed context used for evaluation. |
| Conditions | Evaluated condition states based on the derived features. | Find conditions that need closer review and inspect their context. |

Available categories include device health, interfaces, and network protocols. The signals and charts shown depend on the devices, collection coverage, and selected scope.

## Example: investigate a busy device

Start with the organization view to find a device with elevated CPU measurements. Switch to its site and device, inspect the available device health signals and features, and review any relevant conditions.

Check the measurement times and reporting coverage, then use [Chat](/capabilities/chat) or [Incident analysis](/capabilities/incident-analysis) to continue with a clear device and time scope.

## How to use Monitoring

<Note>
  **Draft guide.** The functionality is grounded in the product source. Validate these steps against your deployed REAP version before publishing this guide to customers.
</Note>

Confirm that the relevant devices are discovered and telemetry is being collected. Inventory presence alone does not establish that every signal is available.

1. Open **Telemetry** to reach **Telemetry Explorer**.
2. Set **Scope** to **Organization** for the organization summary. Choose the relevant **Time range** and review the available coverage, health, and trend information.
3. To investigate a particular area, change **Scope** to **Site and Device**. Select **Site**, then optionally **Role/Layer** and **Device**.
4. Select **Category** and choose **Signals**, **Features**, or **Conditions**.
5. Narrow the results with **Signal Key**, **Feature Key**, or **Condition Key** as appropriate. For conditions, use **State** to view **ON only**, **OFF only**, or **ON + OFF**.
6. Review the available values, timestamps, and related charts. Set **Refresh** to control how often the view requests updated information.

## Understand the result

The organization time range applies to its trend views; the Signals, Features, and Conditions views describe the latest available data for their scope. Check the displayed timestamps rather than assuming every panel represents the same instant.

An absent value, unavailable chart, or missing condition can indicate a collection or coverage gap. It does not establish that a device is healthy. Use monitoring evidence alongside incident context and other checks before deciding what action to take.
