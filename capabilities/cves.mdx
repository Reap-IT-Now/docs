A CVE identifies a publicly disclosed vulnerability. REAP's CVE assessments help you understand how known vulnerabilities relate to the devices in your network.

REAP evaluates available device vendor, product, and software-version information against its vulnerability signatures. When a signature includes configuration conditions, the assessment also checks the collected configuration. This adds device-specific context to a vulnerability review and helps your team focus on the exposures that need investigation.

## What you can do

- **Find relevant assessments.** Review findings by CVE, device, site, severity, or assessment status.
- **Understand the reasoning.** Inspect the assessment's justification and any configuration evidence showing why it was classified.
- **Prepare the next action.** Review available patch guidance, mitigation suggestions, and operational-impact information.
- **Review a point in time.** Use current or historical time selections to examine the assessments available for that period.

The page is called **Vulnerability Assessments** and is reached through **CVEs** in the navigation.

## Example: review a configuration-dependent exposure

A vulnerability may apply to a software release only when a particular service or feature is enabled. Where the CVE signature describes that condition, REAP checks the available configuration and includes the reasoning in the assessment.

Your team can inspect that evidence before choosing a patch or mitigation. This helps separate a version match from a finding supported by the relevant configuration condition.

## How to review CVEs

<Note>
  **Draft guide.** The functionality is grounded in the product source. Validate these steps against your deployed REAP version before publishing this guide to customers.
</Note>

Confirm that the relevant devices, software versions, and required configuration data have been collected.

1. Open **CVEs**.
2. Choose the current or historical time context and find the relevant CVE, device, or site.
3. Open an assessment. Confirm the device, severity, status, and assessment time.
4. Read **Evaluation Justification** and any configuration evidence.
5. Review **Remediation Action** and **Operational Impact Statement** when available.
6. Verify the proposed action for your device and environment, then plan the remediation through your change process.

## Understand the result

**EXPOSED** means the assessment matched the signature's applicable conditions using the available inputs. **AUDITED SAFE** is the UI label for an assessment that did not match those conditions. It describes that CVE assessment, not a guarantee that the device has no vulnerabilities.

Check the software version, configuration completeness, assessment time, and justification before deciding what to do. Missing assessments or missing data do not establish that a device is safe. Remediation suggestions require review against the applicable vendor advisory and your operational requirements.
