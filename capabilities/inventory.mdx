Inventory brings discovered device records into one place so you can establish what is in your network. You can find a device, confirm its identity and site, and inspect the information REAP has collected about it.

This gives your team a starting point for everyday network work. Before investigating a symptom or planning a change, you can confirm that you are working with the intended device and follow its available configuration, port, endpoint, and vulnerability details.

## What you can do

- **Find the right device.** Search by name, vendor, model, or IP address.
- **Confirm its identity and location.** Check the device's site, management address, and available hardware and software details.
- **Inspect collected context.** Review available ports, attached endpoints, configuration, and vulnerability assessments.
- **Continue the investigation.** Use the device identity and site to scope a Chat question, explore topology, or review configuration changes.

Inventory reflects the discovery and collection data available to REAP. A record gives you collected context; check its freshness before treating it as the device's current state.

## Example: identify a branch switch

When a team reports a problem with a branch switch, search for its hostname or management address. Confirm the site and device identity, then inspect the ports and endpoints available in its record.

You can use that scope to ask [Chat](/capabilities/chat) a focused question or explore the surrounding network with [Topology and Path Tracer](/capabilities/topology-and-path-tracer).

## How to use Inventory

<Note>
  **Draft guide.** The functionality is grounded in the product source. Validate these steps against your deployed REAP version before publishing this guide to customers.
</Note>

Complete [site discovery](/deployment/sites-and-discovery) and verify that an expected device appears.

1. Open **Inventory**.
2. Search by name, vendor, model, or IP address.
3. Open the device record and confirm its identity, site, and management address.
4. Review the details available for your question.
5. Follow the configuration or vulnerability assessment links when you need the underlying evidence.

## Understand the result

A missing device can indicate a discovery or collection gap. Check the discovery run, connectivity, and credential coverage before concluding that the device does not exist.
